Jushin “Thunder” Liger had just finished Terry Taylor with a thundering 
Liger Bomb when Brian Pillman put down his headset and got up from the 
commentary table. The crowd at Center Stage went quiet as he stepped through the 
ropes. Only a night earlier in Philadelphia, these two had torn the house down,
and Pillman had stolen the win with a lightning-fast crucifix.

 Pillman stood face 
to face with Liger and held out his hand. Liger paused, looking at it for a 
long moment, then shook it firmly, and the building erupted. Pillman then 
grabbed the microphone. “Philly was the best match of my life,” he said, “and 
I barely got out of there with a win. You deserve another shot, and I want the 
whole world to see it. 

Baltimore. The Great American Bash. You and me.”

Liger didn’t need a translator. 
He nodded once, raised Pillman’s hand to the crowd, and pointed straight down at 
the mat. 
It’s on. The handshake was sincere, but don’t mistake it for friendship. 
Both men walked up the aisle without looking back, and come July 14th only one 
of them will leave Baltimore with bragging rights.
