Match observations: 

LUGER LOCKS IN THE GOLD
WCW Magazine | The Great American Bash '91 | Baltimore Arena, July 14, 1991

For three years Lex Luger was told he was next. On Sunday night in
Baltimore, inside fifteen feet of steel, next finally came. The Total
Package piledrived Barry Windham into the canvas after 23 minutes of war
and walked out of the cage as the first man to wear WCW's new World
Heavyweight Championship.

It was not pretty, and it was not supposed to be. Windham asked for the
cage so nobody could save Luger, and for most of the match he looked like
the smarter man. He bloodied Luger on the steel, dragged him up the corner
and dropped him with a superplex that had 7,000 fans on their feet counting
along. Harley Race, at ringside for the first time as a full-time manager
and nothing else, could only pound on the cage door. Mr. Hughes paced the
floor. When Windham went up again, Luger met him on the top rope, brought
him down, and hooked him for the piledriver. One, two, three. Jim Herd
handed the champion his belt, and Windham, still on the mat, never took his
eyes off it.

"Three years of next time," Luger told the crowd. "Nobody in this company
is ever going to take it away from me." Windham pulled himself up the cage
and pointed at the belt. That one is not finished.

STING HOLDS THE LINE. The United States Champion and Nikita Koloff went 18
minutes in a match that felt like the last chapter of a long book. Koloff
threw everything he had, but Sting caught him in the Scorpion Deathlock and
Koloff had nowhere to go. He submitted, then shook Sting's hand on the way
out to a standing ovation. Sting won't have long to celebrate.

"YOU'RE NEXT." Ravishing Rick Rude beat Rick Steiner with the Rude
Awakening after Paul E. Dangerously got involved behind the referee's back,
then took the microphone and looked straight into the camera. "Sting, enjoy
that pretty little belt while you've got it." Steiner, who saved Dustin
Rhodes from Rude's crew in Greenville the night before, was left holding
his jaw.

THE UPSET OF THE NIGHT. Nobody gave Cactus Jack a chance against Big Van
Vader, and for ten minutes nobody was wrong. Vader threw him around like
laundry. Cactus kept getting up, laughing, bleeding, and asking for more.
Then, out of nowhere, he caught Vader with the double-arm DDT and pinned
the monster in 14:04. Cactus paid for it: he was helped to the back holding
his lower back, and the office says he will be out of action for a while.

THE CIRCLE CRACKS? Cactus's night started with a mess. When El Gigante had
One Man Gang reeling, Cactus came through the crowd with a chair. Gigante
ducked, the chair caught the Gang, and Gigante grabbed the chair just as
the referee turned around. Disqualification. The Gang got the win and gave
Cactus a long look on the way out. The Dark Circle's first big night under
its new leader did not go to plan.

THE Z-MAN SAYS NO. Paul E. introduced Tom Zenk as his newest investment.
Zenk thanked him for the eleven phone calls, tore up the contract in front
of him, and when Bobby Eaton jumped him from behind, dropkicked Eaton to
the floor. Zenk vs. Eaton is coming.

ONE WIN APIECE. Jushin Liger evened the series with Brian Pillman, 1-1,
landing a picture-perfect moonsault after 19 minutes that had the crowd on
its feet. They shook hands again. A rubber match is the only fair answer.

AROUND THE CARD. Richard Morton rolled up his old partner Robert Gibson in
the best match of the undercard, and the York Foundation keeps rolling. Big
Josh pinned Diamond Dallas Page with the Northern Exposure as the Young
Pistols and Josh beat the Freebirds and Page "for Dustin." Paul Orndorff
piledrived the Junkyard Dog and stood over him mocking the crowd. Arn
Anderson and Larry Zbyszko fought to a double count-out and kept brawling
into the aisle, while Paul E. Dangerously watched and took notes. Ron
Simmons flattened Ozz with a spinebuster, and the Bruise Brothers opened
the show by running over PN News and Tommy Rich, one week before the tag
titles are crowned in Johnson City.

WHAT'S NEXT? Saturday Night comes from Johnson City on July 20, where new
World Tag Team Champions are crowned. Luger has the belt. Windham wants it
back. Rude wants Sting. And somewhere, Cactus Jack is laughing.
