BRUISE BROTHERS DEBUT VIGNETTE

Atlanta, GA, Center Stage | 
Segment 6 (24:00–25:30)



Black screen. Rain on a tin roof, then a low, slow bass drum.



FADE IN: grainy, desaturated footage of a dense evergreen forest under heavy 
gray skies, with fog hanging between the trees.



NARRATOR (V.O.): Deep in the Pacific Northwest, where the timber grows tall and 
the winters never end…

Cut to a muddy logging camp. A massive figure in a soaked 
flannel shirt swings an axe into a stump and splits it in one blow. 
That’s Don Harris. He doesn’t look up.



NARRATOR (V.O.): …there are men who were built by hard work and hard living.


Cut to Ron Harris dragging a heavy chain across the mud and wrapping it around 
a fallen log. He hauls it forward by hand, his veins bulging and breath steaming 
in the cold air.



NARRATOR (V.O.): They don’t talk much. They don’t need to.

Cut to both brothers 
standing side by side in front of a beat-up pickup truck, rain running down their
shaved heads. They stare straight into the camera.



DON HARRIS: (low, flat) You heard what we did to the Young Pistols in Philadelphia.



RON HARRIS: That was just the start.

DON HARRIS: We didn’t come to WCW to make 
friends.



RON HARRIS: We came to leave marks.

Don slams his fist into the hood of the truck 
and leaves a deep dent. Neither brother blinks.



BOTH: (together) Bruises heal. We don’t stop.

Hard cut to black. White block 
letters punch onto the screen one word at a time:

THE. BRUISE. BROTHERS.


The drum hits once more. Silence.



TONY SCHIAVONE (live, cutting back to the arena): JR, I don’t know about you, 
but I wouldn’t want to meet those two in a dark alley, or anywhere else.



JIM ROSS: Well, Tony, two men are about to meet them right here. Bill Irwin and
Butch Reed, and good luck to ’em. Here come the Bruise Brothers!
