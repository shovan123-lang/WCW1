Match observations: 

FROM THE PAGES OF WCW MAGAZINE


WCW Saturday Night | July 6th, 1991

Atlanta, GA | Center Stage
Attendance: 1,050 |AVG Ticket Price: $10.00
Gate $10,500 Broadcast: TBS Superstation

THE THRONE SITS EMPTY


WCW Saturday Night opened like no other show in company history. The World 
Heavyweight Championship sat alone in the center of the ring, with no one 
holding it. WCW President Jim Herd made it official: Ric Flair is gone, the title 
is vacant, and the next World Champion will be crowned inside a 
steel cage at The Great American Bash in Baltimore. 

The Center Stage crowd went silent, then the noise built. 
Somebody is going to walk out of Baltimore as the man, 
and that question hung over the whole night.

Liger Bounces Back!


Twenty-four hours after a heartbreaking loss to Brian Pillman in Philadelphia, 
Jushin “Thunder” Liger came out with something to prove. 
Terry Taylor gave him a real fight, but Liger’s speed was too much. 
He finished it with a picture-perfect Liger Bomb. Pillman called the match 
from ringside, then climbed through the ropes and offered his hand. 
Liger shook it, and Pillman offered him a rematch in Baltimore. 
Liger accepted on the spot. Philadelphia’s classic now gets a sequel on the 
biggest stage of the summer.

The Bruise Brothers Leave a Mark!


WCW fans got their first real look at Ron and Don Harris in a debut vignette,
two giants from the Pacific Northwest with nothing but bad intentions. 
Then they showed it in the ring. Bill Irwin and Butch Reed never stood a chance 
in a demolition that was over almost as soon as it started. The Harris brothers 
weren’t done, though. They kept beating on Irwin after the bell until PN News 
and Tommy Rich charged the ring to stop it. The Bruise Brothers just smiled and 
backed up the aisle. That fight is coming in Baltimore.



Mr. Wonderful Has a Message


A profile package reminded everyone just who Paul Orndorff is, and then the man
himself took the microphone. He was still glowing over his upset of Rick Steiner 
and called himself “the best thing to happen to WCW in years.” That was enough 
for the Junk Yard Dog. JYD stormed the interview area, still angry after losing to
Arn Anderson in Philly, and demanded Orndorff face him. 
Orndorff laughed in his face: “Baltimore, Dog… if you can still walk.” It’s on.



Betrayal! 
The Express Is Derailed! 

Nobody saw it coming. The Rock ’n’ Roll Express looked 
like their old selves against Bobby Eaton and Diamond Dallas Page, and 
Robert Gibson scored the pin on DDP to a big ovation. Then, as Gibson turned to 
celebrate with his partner of nearly a decade, Ricky Morton attacked him from 
behind. Morton beat Gibson down to a stunned crowd and walked off without a word 
of explanation. The two of them meet one-on-one at the Bash, and the Express will 
never be the same.



The Freebirds Fly Again


There was good news on the medical front. Michael Hayes has been cleared to return,
and he’ll be back in action in Columbus on Monday night. 
Jimmy Garvin, who’s been holding down the fort alone, couldn’t hide his excitement:
“Columbus, you’d better be ready. The Freebirds fly again!

”

Ravishing Rick Rude Sets His Sights
 
In a taped message, 
Rick Rude went right after the United States Champion. He made it clear he’ll beat
Rick Steiner in Baltimore first. After that, Rude said, Sting’s U.S. Title belongs 
around the waist of the “sexiest man alive. 

”

The Dark Circle Grows Darker"


The lights went down as Kevin Sullivan spoke from the shadows, with Mr. Hughes 
and One Man Gang behind him. His message was cryptic and chilling: the Dark Circle
is coming for everyone. Sullivan backed it up against Dustin Rhodes. Dustin had 
Sullivan in trouble until Hughes and OMG hit the ring and caused the 
disqualification. The three-on-one beating looked like it would go on until the 
arena shook. El Gigante charged to the ring, cleared the Dark Circle, and threw 
One Man Gang over the top rope. Gigante and OMG will settle it in Baltimore.



The Road to Baltimore" 


A special look back at Bash history set the stakes for next Sunday. 
In 1988, Lex Luger came within an inch of the World Title against Ric Flair 
in the Baltimore Arena and came up short. In 1990, Sting won the gold in that 
same building. Baltimore is where champions are made. Will Luger finally finish 
what he started three years ago, or will Barry Windham write his own chapter?
 


Arn and Larry Can’t Wait

 
Larry Zbyszko called Arn Anderson’s win over JYD “dumb luck,” and the Enforcer 
didn’t take it well. Words turned into fists, and it took a group of officials 
to pull the two veterans apart. They’ll finish it at the Bash.



Nikita and Sting Face to Face


Still smarting from his loss to Lex Luger, Nikita Koloff came face to face with
Sting and promised to take his frustration out on the U.S. Champion in Baltimore.
Sting didn’t back down an inch. These two have history, and Baltimore will add a 
new chapter.



Windham Throws Down the Gauntlet!


The main event was a hard-hitting war. Barry Windham and Ozz fought back and forth,
with Ron Simmons watching closely from ringside. Windham finally hit a devastating
superplex for the win. Ozz attacked him after the bell, and Simmons stormed in and 
cleaned house, sending Ozz running. Simmons and Ozz will meet in Baltimore.



The night wasn’t over. Lex Luger walked out on the stage with Harley Race 
and Mr. Hughes at his side. Windham grabbed the microphone and challenged Luger,
inside the cage, for the vacant World Heavyweight Championship. Luger smirked. 
Harley Race stepped forward and accepted for him. Windham and Luger stared 
each other down as Saturday Night went off the air.



Next stop: WCW rolls into the Richmond Coliseum tomorrow night, with 
Sting & Barry Windham teaming against Lex Luger & Mr. Hughes. 
Then it’s Columbus, Georgia on Monday, where Michael Hayes returns and the 
Freebirds reunite. The Great American Bash is just eight days away!
