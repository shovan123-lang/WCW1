RON SIMMONS MAKES THE SAVE / WINDHAM CALLS OUT LUGER
Atlanta, GA, Center Stage | Segment 23 (1:54:00–2:00:00)

Barry Windham hits the superplex off the top rope and the whole ring shakes. He
hooks Ozz's leg. One, two, three. Windham's music hits and the Center Stage crowd
roars.

JIM ROSS: Barry Windham wins! What a superplex, Tony! Eight days before the
biggest match of his life, Barry Windham is ready!

Windham gets to his feet, exhausted, and the referee raises his hand. Behind him,
Ozz rolls to the apron, pulls himself up and comes back through the ropes. He
blindsides Windham with a forearm to the back of the head. Windham goes down hard.

TONY SCHIAVONE: Oh, come on! The match is over!

Ozz stomps Windham into the corner, then drags him to the center of the ring and
sets him up for a powerbomb. At ringside, Ron Simmons has seen enough. He tears
off his jacket and slides into the ring.

JIM ROSS: Here comes Ron Simmons! He's been watching all night!

Simmons spins Ozz around and drops him with a thunderous spinebuster. The crowd
explodes. Ozz rolls out to the floor, clutching his back, and Simmons stands at
the ropes pointing down at him.

RON SIMMONS: (shouting) Baltimore! You and me, Ozz! BALTIMORE!

Ozz backs up the aisle, glaring at Simmons. Simmons turns and helps Windham to his
feet. The two men shake hands as the crowd cheers.

TONY SCHIAVONE: Ron Simmons and Ozz at the Great American Bash, JR. Simmons is
gonna get his hands on him.

Windham asks the timekeeper for a microphone. Simmons steps out of the ring to give
him the spotlight. Windham stands alone in the center of the ring, breathing hard,
and looks up toward the entrance stage.

BARRY WINDHAM: Earlier tonight, Jim Herd stood in this ring and told the world
that on July 14th, me and Lex Luger are going into a steel cage for the World
Heavyweight Championship. Well, that's fine with me. That's business.

(He wipes sweat from his face.)

BARRY WINDHAM: But I want everybody to understand something. This isn't business
for me anymore. This is personal. Lex, you've been walkin' around this company
like you already own that title. You've got Harley Race whisperin' in your ear and
Mr. Hughes watchin' your back. So I'm gonna ask you one time, man to man. Come out
here and look me in the eye.

The crowd starts chanting "LUGER! LUGER!" mixed with boos. A beat passes. Then
Lex Luger's music hits.

Luger walks out onto the entrance stage with Harley Race on one side and Mr.
Hughes on the other. He's in street clothes, arms folded, wearing a smug smile.
Race holds a microphone. Hughes stares at the ring.

JIM ROSS: There he is. Lex Luger, with Harley Race and Mr. Hughes.

HARLEY RACE: Windham, you want to talk man to man? My man doesn't need to come down
to that ring. He's got nothing to prove to you tonight.

BARRY WINDHAM: Then let HIM say it, Race!

Luger takes the microphone from Race.

LEX LUGER: You want me to look you in the eye, Barry? (He takes off his
sunglasses.) Here I am. Look all you want. Because eight days from now, it's the
last thing you're gonna see before I put you down for good.

LEX LUGER: Three years ago, Baltimore took that title away from me. Not this time.
This time I've got the best mind in the business in my corner, I've got the
toughest bodyguard alive watchin' my back, and in that cage, Windham, there's no
referee to save you and no door to run through.

BARRY WINDHAM: That's right, Lex. No door. No Race. No Hughes. Just you and me and
fifteen feet of steel. And when that cage comes down, I'm gonna remind the whole
world why they've been waitin' for me.

(Windham drops the microphone and points straight at Luger.)

Luger hands the microphone back to Race and steps to the front of the stage. He
holds his arm up and slowly mimes a belt being strapped around his waist. The
crowd boos. Windham doesn't move.

Race grabs Luger's shoulder and turns him away. Hughes stands at the edge of the
stage, staring at Windham, as Luger and Race walk through the curtain. Hughes is
the last to leave, still watching.

Windham stands alone in the middle of the ring, chest heaving, eyes locked on the
entrance. Simmons stands at ringside behind him. The crowd is on its feet.

JIM ROSS: Tony, the words are done. In eight days, it's Luger and Windham inside a
steel cage, and somebody walks out of Baltimore as the new World Heavyweight
Champion.

TONY SCHIAVONE: For JR, I'm Tony Schiavone. We'll see you at the Great American
Bash!

Freeze-frame on Windham in the ring, staring up the aisle. Fade to black.
