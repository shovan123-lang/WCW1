Match observations: 

WCW Pro Wrestling, Episode 2
Taped: Tallahassee, FL, 7/9/1991
Studio: CNN Center, Atlanta, GA
Aired: Saturday, 7/20/1991, syndication
Attendance: 5,200 (taping crowd, counted with Episode 1)
Average ticket: $12.00
Gate: $62,400 (Episode 1 and 2 taping)

The Television Title tournament reached its final four this week, and
the second hour of tape from Tallahassee belonged to Tommy Rich. The
Wildfire made Bobby Eaton give up to the sleeper in a 14-minute main
event, and he did it with Paul E. Dangerously's whole operation coming
apart at ringside.

Jim Ross and Tony Schiavone opened from Atlanta with the bracket on the
screen. Paul Orndorff and Rick Steiner advanced last week. Two more
spots were on the line today.

The Bruise Brothers kept their momentum going into tonight's tag team
tournament. Eli the Eliminator was held up on the road and never made
the building, so the Junk Yard Dog stepped in to team with Duke Myers
on short notice. JYD gave the Harris twins a fight for a few minutes,
but Myers got caught alone and Ron Harris flattened him with the big
boot at 4:28. Two big men, two big boots, and a semifinal tonight
against the Freebirds.

The desk then ran back a brutal week. Footage from Greenville showed
Rick Rude dropping an already injured Dustin Rhodes neck first with the
Rude Awakening while Paul E. stood over him smiling, until Rick Steiner
ran Rude and Eaton off. Dustin is out with no return date. From there
it was Baltimore, where Lex Luger beat Barry Windham in the cage for the
vacant World Title, then Cleveland four days later, where Nikita Koloff
choked Luger out with the front facelock to become champion. Last night
in Charleston, Rude beat Sting for the United States Title. Paul E. now
has a champion, and he wants more.

TV Title Quarterfinal: Larry Zbyszko pinned Robert Gibson with the
brainbuster at 6:18. Gibson came in fired up and had Zbyszko reeling
early with his speed, but the Living Legend did what he does: he slowed
it down, worked the head and waited. Once Gibson ran into a knee coming
off the ropes, Zbyszko set him up and finished it with one brainbuster.
Workmanlike and effective. Zbyszko is in the semifinals and told anyone
who would listen that the belt was always his.

The ugliest moment of the hour came next. Brian Blair and Jim Brunzell
were at ringside talking about the tag team title picture when Dick
Murdoch and Butch Reed came over the barricade. Reed sent Brunzell into
the ring post, and Murdoch ripped the mask off Blair's head and threw
it into the crowd. Blair stayed on his knees with his face in his hands
while Murdoch laughed on his way up the aisle. The Bees beat Murdoch and
Reed with the old mask switch. This was payback, and it won't end here.

The Young Pistols tuned up for tonight with a fast win over the
Assassins. Tracy Smothers and Steve Armstrong never let the masked men
settle in, kept the tags quick, and Armstrong finished Assassin #2 with
a flying clothesline at 5:08. The Pistols meet the York Foundation in
the second semifinal tonight in Johnson City.

Cactus Jack sat alone on the apron in the dark and lit Kevin Sullivan's
black candle. Sullivan is gone and Mr. Hughes walked out after him, but
Jack says the Dark Circle did not die with them. He's carrying the
flame now, and he made an open call to the big, mean men in the back
who don't fit anywhere else. The circle is open. That was taped before
Baltimore, where Jack pinned Big Van Vader and hurt his back doing it.

JR and Tony previewed tonight's WCW Saturday Night from Johnson City,
where the first WCW World Tag Team Champions will be crowned: the
Freebirds with Diamond Dallas Page against the Bruise Brothers, and the
Young Pistols against Terry Taylor and Richard Morton, with the winners
meeting for the belts. New World Champion Nikita Koloff will be live,
Ron Simmons meets Mr. Hughes, Vader faces PN News, Barry Windham
returns to action, and Paul E. promises to present Rick Rude.

TV Title Quarterfinal: Tommy Rich beat Bobby Eaton by submission with
the sleeperhold at 14:12. The best match of the hour, and it was not
close. Eaton controlled long stretches on Rich's left arm, wrenching it
into the post and the mat, while Paul E. barked orders from the floor
with the cellular phone in his hand. Rich kept coming back on pure
fire. Late in the match Paul E. climbed onto the apron to give Eaton an
opening, and Brian Pillman and Tom Zenk came down to even things up.
They pulled Paul E. off the apron and cut Eaton off when he went for
the phone. In the scramble, Rich ducked a clothesline, locked in the
sleeper and dragged Eaton to the mat. Eaton fought it for a long time
before he gave up. Paul E. threw the phone at the ring and blamed
everyone but himself.

At the close, JR said Paul E.'s investment had just lost money, and
Tony said Pillman and Zenk weren't about to let Paul E. steal another
one. The final four is set for next week's WCW Pro: Paul Orndorff
against Larry Zbyszko, and Rick Steiner against Tommy Rich. One of
those four men will be the first WCW Television Champion.

Card rating: **3/4
