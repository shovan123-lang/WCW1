Match observations: 

WCW MAGAZINE: AFTER THE BASH
Salisbury, Maryland | Wicomico Youth Center | July 15, 1991

STING CONQUERS THE MASTODON!

The Great Muta never made it to Salisbury. The fans who packed the
Wicomico Youth Center to see him challenge Sting got something nobody
expected instead: Big Van Vader, walking through the curtain the night
after Baltimore, still angry about losing to Cactus Jack and looking for
somebody to take it out on.

For nineteen and a half minutes the U.S. Champion and the 400-pound
Mastodon tore the building apart. Vader threw Sting out of the ring,
powerbombed him, hit him with the Vader Bomb not once but twice and came
off the top with a moonsault that shook the whole Eastern Shore. Every
time Vader went for the cover, Sting kicked out, and every time Sting
kicked out the crowd got louder. Fans were throwing cups into the ring by
the end of it.

Then came the moment that will have people talking all week. With Sting
in control and Vader on the floor, Harley Race came from behind and
distracted the champion. Yes, that Harley Race, the man in Lex Luger's
corner. Vader took advantage, but he couldn't put Sting away. Vader went
for one more forearm, Sting blocked it, and the Scorpion Deathlock went on
in the middle of the ring. Vader fought it for thirteen seconds. Then he
gave up.

Sting made Big Van Vader submit at 19:29, and the chants in Salisbury
were deafening. The title was not on the line. It didn't need to be.

What was Harley Race doing out there? Race managed Luger to the World
Title one night earlier. Is he looking for a second client, or was he
sending a message to the man who owns the United States Title? Sting and
Vader meet again Sunday in Knoxville, and we'll be watching the aisle.

THE Z-MAN DRAWS FIRST BLOOD

One night after he tore up Paul E. Dangerously's contract on
pay-per-view, Tom Zenk got his hands on Bobby Eaton. Eaton nearly ended it
with the Alabama Jam, and Zenk kicked out at two and nine-tenths. Moments
later Eaton went for a kick, Zenk caught the leg and spun him to the mat
with a dragon screw, then came off the top with a flying cross body for
the three count at 12:45. Paul E. stood in the aisle screaming at his
investment. Round one goes to the Z-Man.

ARN WINS, BUT AT WHAT COST?

Big Josh had the Salisbury crowd firmly behind him against Arn Anderson,
and when Paul E. Dangerously crept up behind him, the lumberjack turned
around and decked him. Later Paul E. tried to toss Arn his portable
phone, and Josh snatched it out of the air. Arn didn't need it. He
finished Josh with a single-arm DDT and two more DDTs after that, pinning
him at 14:10.

Big Josh didn't walk out. He tore the quadriceps muscle in his left leg
and will be out of action for a long time. As for Arn, he never asked for
Paul E.'s help. He didn't turn it down either.

ROCK 'N' ROLL REVENGE

Robert Gibson didn't get Richard Morton tonight, but he got the next best
thing. Terry Taylor came after him with everything he had, and Gibson
fought back with a monkey flip, a reverse neckbreaker and a roll-up that
caught Taylor at 8:08. The crowd went into a frenzy. Gibson lost to Morton
at the Bash in one of the best matches of the year. Tonight he made the
York Foundation pay a little bit back.

AROUND THE RING

Jushin Liger soared over Diamond Dallas Page with the moonsault at 13:04.
El Gigante got his receipt for the Bash, chokeslamming One Man Gang in
5:10, and this time there was no Cactus Jack and no chair. PN News
flattened Ozz with the Broken Record at 7:30. And the Young Pistols opened
the night by beating Bill Irwin and Black Bart when Tracy Smothers pinned
Bart after a dropkick.
