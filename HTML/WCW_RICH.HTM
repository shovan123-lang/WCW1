WRESTLING OBSERVER-STYLE RECAP
WCW Richmond, VA | Richmond Coliseum | July 7, 1991

7/7 Richmond, VA (Coliseum): 6,000 fans paid an average of $12 for a $72,000
gate. That's a good number for a Sunday house show, especially in the first week
without Ric Flair. WCW pushed this show harder than any other on Saturday's TBS
broadcast, with local promos from Sting, Windham, Orndorff and Pillman, and it
looks like the push worked. For comparison, Philadelphia drew 10,000 at $20 two
nights earlier for $200,000, but that was a premium show with a much stronger
card on paper. The lower ticket price in Richmond kept the house full, and the
question going forward is whether WCW can keep crowds this size once the
novelty of the vacant title wears off. The card came in around **1/2. That's a
step up from the Atlanta TV taping, though well off the Philly show. It was an
uneven card: one great match, a couple of good ones and a lot of filler.

The Young Pistols beat Richard Morton & Terry Taylor in 12:46 when Smothers
rolled up Taylor. **. Morton and Taylor were billed as the York Foundation, so
the plan for Morton's heel turn is clearly to group him with Taylor rather than
have him float around as a single. Morton refusing the tag and costing his own
team was a smart way to establish him as selfish, but the crowd hasn't caught up
to the turn yet and didn't react to him the way they should have. The Pistols
needed the win after being squashed by the Bruise Brothers in Philly.

El Gigante pinned Butch Reed with a chokeslam in 5:07. 1/2*. About what you'd
expect. Gigante is over as an attraction because of the size, but there isn't
much there in the ring, and five minutes is about the limit.

Brian Pillman pinned Bobby Eaton with a flying cross body in 17:05. ***1/4.
Very good match. The interesting angle was Paul E. Dangerously getting involved
on Eaton's behalf. Paul E. and Eaton together is a pairing that makes too much
sense to be a one-time thing. Pillman has had two strong matches in three nights
and is a few days away from the Liger rematch at the Bash, which should steal the
show.

Arn Anderson pinned Big Josh with the DDT in 12:09. *1/4. Arn is as good as
anyone in the company, but Josh just isn't at that level, and twelve minutes was
too long. This should have been seven minutes.

The Bruise Brothers beat the Killer Bees in 12:03 when Don Harris pinned
Brunzell with the big boot. *3/4. Their best match so far in WCW, which isn't
saying much, but it's a clear improvement over the two squashes that rated in
the negatives. Giving them experienced opponents and real time helped. They're
still a work in progress.

After intermission, Paul Orndorff pinned Tommy Rich with the piledriver in
12:25. ****1/4. Easily the match of the night and a big surprise. Rich has been
a mid-card babyface for a long time, but he and Orndorff had great chemistry,
and the 6,000 fans were into every minute of it. Rick Steiner came out and
interfered against Orndorff, which keeps the Steiner issue alive after Orndorff
beat him in Philly. The catch is that Steiner is booked against Rude at the Bash
and Orndorff has JYD, so this probably goes on the back burner until after
Baltimore. Orndorff has delivered two of the best matches on the tour in his
first three nights back. He's the hottest heel in the company right now that
isn't named Luger.

Rick Rude beat Dustin Rhodes by count-out in 17:58. **1/4. Rude flew in from
Japan for this, and he looked a little slow. Paul E. Dangerously got involved
again, this time against Dustin, which is now two interferences in one night on
behalf of two different heels. That's a stable being put together. Rude winning
by count-out protects Dustin, who has now lost or been used in angles on three
straight shows.

Main event saw Sting & Barry Windham beat Lex Luger & Mr. Hughes in 20:20 when
Sting made Hughes submit to the Scorpion Deathlock. **3/4. Harley Race got
involved against Windham, but the babyfaces won clean in the end. That's a
change from the planned DQ finish. In some ways it's better, because Luger is
protected (Hughes took the fall) while the babyfaces sent the Richmond fans home
happy. Luger was solid but held back, which makes sense a week out from the
cage. Windham continues to look like a credible challenger.

Big picture: The business story is encouraging. A 6,000 house in Richmond the
first weekend after Flair's departure suggests the Bash build on TBS is working,
and the Luger and Windham main drew without the title in either man's hands. The
creative story is Paul E. Dangerously showing up twice on the same show, for
Eaton and for Rude. Add the York Foundation formation and Steiner's
interference on Orndorff, and WCW has several new threads moving at once with
the Bash a week away. The concern going in was whether the company could hold
houses without Flair. One night in Richmond doesn't answer that, but this was a
good start, and the in-ring product was strong when the right people were in
the ring.
