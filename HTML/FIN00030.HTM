RICK MORTON TURNS ON ROBERT GIBSON
Atlanta, GA, Center Stage | Segment 12 (48:30–50:30)

Robert Gibson hooks Diamond Dallas Page's leg after the Rock 'n' Roll Express
double dropkick. The referee counts one, two, three, and Center Stage comes
unglued. The Express's music hits. Gibson rolls out of the ring, slaps hands with
the front row, then climbs back in and throws his arms around his partner.

JIM ROSS: There they are, Tony! The Rock 'n' Roll Express, still one of the most
beloved teams in the history of this sport!

TONY SCHIAVONE: Almost ten years together, JR, and they never miss a beat.

Gibson climbs to the second rope in the corner and pumps his fist to the crowd.
Behind him, Rick Morton stands in the middle of the ring, clapping slowly. The
smile is gone from his face.

JIM ROSS: Ricky's... Ricky's not celebrating, Tony.

Gibson hops down and turns around, arms open for another hug. Morton drives a
knee straight into his stomach. Gibson doubles over, and the crowd gasps.

TONY SCHIAVONE: What? What was THAT?

Morton grabs Gibson by the hair and slams his face into the top turnbuckle. Once.
Twice. Three times. Gibson crumples to the mat. Morton stomps him over and over as
boos pour down from every corner of the building.

JIM ROSS: No! NO! Ricky Morton has turned on Robert Gibson! Tony, I can't believe
what I'm seeing!

TONY SCHIAVONE: His own partner! His best friend!

Morton drags Gibson to the center of the ring and drops him with a DDT. He stands
over his former partner, chest heaving, then calls for a microphone. The boos are
deafening.

RICK MORTON: (breathing hard) Ten years. Ten years I carried you, Robert. Ten years
of everybody screaming "Rock 'n' Roll Express" like we were one man. Well, I'm
done sharing. I'm done being half of anything.

(He looks down at Gibson.)

RICK MORTON: And from now on, don't you DARE call me Ricky. Ricky was a little boy
who did what he was told. My name... is RICHARD Morton.

He throws the microphone down onto Gibson's chest and walks out. He doesn't look
back. Fans throw cups toward the aisle. Officials hit the ring to check on Gibson,
who is clutching his face and still hasn't moved.

JIM ROSS: "Richard" Morton. Tony, ten years of friendship, thrown away in ten
seconds. I've been in this business a long time and I am sick to my stomach.

TONY SCHIAVONE: Robert Gibson never saw it coming. None of us did.

JIM ROSS: You know what, Tony? Robert Gibson's gonna get up from this. And when he
does, he's gonna want answers. And I hope he gets them in Baltimore.

Cut to break.
