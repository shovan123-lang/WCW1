WRESTLING OBSERVER-STYLE RECAP
WCW Columbus, GA | Columbus Civic Center | July 8, 1991

7/8 Columbus, GA (Civic Center): 4,500 fans paid an average of $15 for a $67,500
gate in a building that holds 10,000, so it was less than half full. That's a
disappointing number for a Monday show that was advertised on TBS with the
Freebirds' reunion as the hook. For comparison, Richmond drew 6,000 at $12 the
night before, so WCW charged more and drew less. It's the first sign that the
post-Flair houses may not hold up once you get away from the big markets. The card
came in around **1/2, with a terrific Liger vs. Dustin Rhodes match carrying the
first half and the Freebirds' return carrying the second. But the bigger stories
from Columbus happened outside the ring, and both of them will change WCW's plans
going into the Bash.

The biggest news is that Kevin Sullivan quit the promotion after the show,
effective immediately. Here's the story going around the locker room. During the
Dark Circle's taped promo in Atlanta, one of Sullivan's candles tipped over and
singed part of the backdrop at Center Stage. Word got back to Jim Herd, who fined
Sullivan for it and then told him the candles were banned for the rest of the
tour because of fire marshal concerns. Sullivan reportedly argued that the candles
were "the whole gimmick" and that you can't run a cult without fire. Herd
reportedly told him to "use a flashlight." Sullivan worked the six-man in Columbus,
had his gear packed before the main event was over, and was gone that night. One
wrestler said the last thing Sullivan said on his way out was that he'd "find a
place that appreciates atmosphere."

As for where he lands, the most obvious rumor is the USWA. Sullivan has deep ties
to the Memphis territory, and with Terry Funk's Texas Bunch already causing chaos
there, the idea of Sullivan joining them has been talked about. There's also talk
of Mexico. CMLL has rudo factions forming around Satánico, and a Sullivan-style
leader would fit right in there. Nobody has confirmed anything, but don't expect
him to sit at home for long.

WCW's plan for the Dark Circle is said to be Cactus Jack taking over the group,
built slowly on WCW Pro. That fits, since Cactus has been off TV and the office
wants to get him back into the mix. Mr. Hughes, who has been working both sides
as a Dark Circle member and Luger's bodyguard, will reportedly go with Luger and
Harley Race full time.

The other big story is Dustin Rhodes. He and Jushin Liger went twenty minutes to a
draw. ****. Best match of the night by a wide margin, and one of the best on the
tour. Somewhere in the second half Dustin took a bump wrong and hurt his neck. He
finished the match, but he was in a lot of pain afterward. The initial estimate
was a couple of weeks, but the word now is that he's out until the fall. He comes
off the Bash six-man against the Freebirds. Here's the interesting part. The
plan is reportedly to turn the injury into an angle, with Paul E. Dangerously's
group being blamed for putting him out on Saturday's TBS show in Greenville. If
that's true, it gives Dustin a strong comeback story when he returns and gives
Paul E.'s crew real heat right away. Dustin's family is said to be concerned but
supportive of the plan, and Dustin wants it known that he plans to come back
"meaner than ever."

Bobby Eaton pinned Tracy Smothers with the Alabama Jam in 12:12. ***. Good opener.
Paul E. got involved again for Eaton. That's three interferences in two nights,
two of them for Eaton, and it's becoming clear that Paul E. is putting together
a group. Eaton has been one of the most underrated wrestlers in the company for
years, and he looks re-energized.

Larry Zbyszko pinned Butch Reed with the brainbuster in 8:40. **3/4. Solid
undercard match. Zbyszko and Arn were kept apart ahead of their Bash match.

Abdullah the Butcher pinned Bill Irwin with an elbow drop in 6:12. -**1/4. This
was bad. Abdullah is still a big name, but he can't carry a match against
somebody who isn't at his level, and Irwin couldn't fill the gaps. If Abdullah is
going to be in a program with Sting in August, as rumored, it will need short
matches with lots of brawling and gimmicks. He's also back to Japan this week for
All Japan.

Arn Anderson pinned Steve Armstrong with a double-arm DDT in 10:20. **. Fine.

After intermission, the Dark Circle (Sullivan, Hughes and One Man Gang) beat
Robert Gibson, PN News and Tommy Rich when Hughes pinned Rich with a sidewalk slam
in 12:44. *1/4. In hindsight, this was Sullivan's last WCW match. Nobody in the
building knew it at the time.

The Fabulous Freebirds beat the Killer Bees in 12:36 when Michael Hayes pinned
Blair with the DDT. ***3/4. Hayes looked great in his return from injury, and the
crowd was hot for it. The Freebirds are still one of the best tag teams in the
company when they're on, and they should be a factor once the tag titles are
crowned in Johnson City on 7/20.

Sting beat Ozz in 12:16 when Ozz passed out in the Scorpion Deathlock. **3/4. This
was billed as a U.S. Title defense. Sting carried it.

Main event saw Rick Steiner & Nikita Koloff beat Big Van Vader & Great Muta in
20:55 when Steiner pinned Muta with the Steiner Bulldog. ***. A good, hard-hitting
main. Steiner and Koloff both lost in Philly, and both needed a win going into
the Bash. Vader and Muta have now lost back-to-back main events, which is
something to keep an eye on. Both are too valuable to keep losing.

A note on Rick Rude. He wasn't in Columbus, because he's on his way back to Japan
for All Japan's show in Nagoya on 7/11. Rude has been bouncing back and forth
across the Pacific all month: Sendai on 7/5, Richmond on 7/7, Nagoya on 7/11, then
back for the Bash on 7/14, then Kumamoto on 7/17. When asked about the schedule
before leaving Richmond, Rude reportedly said, "Sweathogs complain about jet lag.
I don't get jet lag. Jet lag gets Rick Rude." He also joked that he's been
on so many flights the stewardesses "should be paying me for the privilege." He
reportedly told Paul E. that he'd fly to the moon if it meant getting a shot at
Sting. Rude vs. Rick Steiner is scheduled for the Bash.

Bottom line: WCW lost a stable leader and an injured babyface in one night, six
days before its biggest show of the summer, and drew a house that filled less
than half the building. The three shows so far tell the story. Philly drew
10,000 as a premium event, Richmond did 6,000, and Columbus did 4,500 at a
higher price. The Bash card is being reshuffled, and how the company handles the
next few days, especially the Greenville TV show, will say a lot about whether
WCW can sell tickets without Flair.
