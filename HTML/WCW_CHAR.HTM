WCW MAGAZINE: ROAD TO THE BASH
Charleston, South Carolina | McAllister Fieldhouse | July 12, 1991

STING MAKES THE ENFORCER PASS OUT!

Two nights before he defends the United States Title against Nikita
Koloff in Baltimore, Sting sent a message the whole Four Horsemen legacy
will feel. For nearly twenty-two minutes Arn Anderson went after the
champion's knee, his back and his patience, and for most of that time
it looked like the Enforcer had a plan. Then Sting caught him, turned
him over and locked in the Scorpion Deathlock. Arn wouldn't give up.
He passed out instead, and the referee called it at 21:43.

Sting stood in the middle of the ring with the belt over his shoulder
and pointed toward Baltimore. Nikita, he's ready.

WINDHAM GETS HIS RECEIPT

One night after Mr. Hughes cost him in Columbia, Barry Windham got the
big man alone. Harley Race barked from the floor and Lex Luger watched
every move from ringside, but Windham wouldn't be stopped. He climbed
to the top rope, brought all four hundred pounds of Hughes down with a
superplex and covered him in the middle of the ring at 14:45. Then he
looked straight at Luger. Neither man said a word. They don't need to.
On Sunday they'll be locked inside a steel cage with the WCW World
Title hanging above them.

STEINER STOPS THE MUTA

Rick Steiner and the Great Muta tore into each other for over fifteen
minutes, with Steiner tossing the Japanese star across the ring and
Muta answering with kicks and that terrifying mist. In the end the
Dog-Faced Gremlin was too strong, pinning Muta at 15:27. That's a big
win heading into Sunday, when Steiner faces Ravishing Rick Rude.

PILLMAN FLIES AGAIN

Brian Pillman needed to bounce back after his loss to Bobby Eaton in
Columbia, and he did. With Jimmy Garvin missing from the building,
Diamond Dallas Page stepped in alongside Michael Hayes, and it was
Page who paid for it. Pillman and Tommy Rich kept the pace high, and
Flyin' Brian came off the top with a cross body to pin Page at 14:39.

PAUL E. IS EVERYWHERE

Paul E. Dangerously had a busy night. First he made sure Bobby Eaton
beat Robert Gibson, cracking Gibson with that portable phone before
Eaton finished him with the Alabama Jam. Later he showed up during
Ron Simmons vs. Larry Zbyszko and tried to tip that one too. It didn't
work. Simmons shrugged it off and planted Zbyszko with the powerslam.
Why does Paul E. care about Larry Zbyszko? Arn Anderson might want to
know before Sunday.

AROUND THE RING

Richard Morton was a no-show, so Jushin Liger faced Terry Taylor
instead and stopped the York Foundation member with the moonsault in a
16:46 thriller. Mr. Wonderful Paul Orndorff dropped Big Josh with the
piledriver. Cactus Jack went wild with Black Bart before finishing him
with the double-arm DDT. And the Young Pistols opened the show with a
win over Bill Irwin and Butch Reed when Tracy Smothers pinned Irwin
after a dropkick.
